A single unbroken 10-second drone take. One camera, one continuous move, filmed in one go. There is no edit, no cut, no montage, no shot change and no transition anywhere in it: the picture runs without interruption from the first frame to the last. This is one shot, not a sequence of shots.

THE MOVE
A camera flies low and forward over a frozen fjord at dawn, and it never stops moving forward. It opens gliding past a glowing white circular podium floating on the mirror-calm water with a giant glossy white molar tooth with four roots hovering just above it; the podium and the tooth slide past on the left and out of the picture after about two seconds and never come back. In the same unbroken move the camera keeps going and banks gently to the right like an aircraft, the horizon tilting about eighteen degrees, slowing down as it curves toward a dark wet granite cliff standing on the right — almost vertical, cut by deep fissures, with thin ledges of snow, its base plunging straight into the water. For about two seconds it glides past that cliff within a few metres of the rock, at its slowest, so the wet granite fills the whole right half of the picture and its cracks are readable. Still without interruption it rolls out of that bank, rolls the other way into a wide left-hand turn and speeds up through the turn — and while the granite is still sliding out of the right edge, a smooth glacier-carved white snow dome is already sliding into the left edge, so there is never a moment with no land in the picture. The camera slows again and for about two seconds glides past that snow dome within a few metres, its rounded white slope and pale blue waterline ice filling the whole left half of the picture. Then, still in the same unbroken move, it rolls level, speeds up a little and flies straight into a low bank of thick fog, which closes over the lens completely, and the take ends held on flat featureless pale blue-white haze with nothing recognisable left in it — no rock, no water, no horizon, no silhouette.

WHAT NEVER CHANGES
The mirror water fills the bottom of the picture in every frame until the fog. The dawn light comes from the upper left the whole time and never changes colour, angle or brightness. The palette is cool white and pale blue only. The two headlands keep exactly the same shape, size and side. The water stays glassy and calm. There is only ever one camera, and it is never repositioned, re-framed or teleported — if a moment feels like it wants a cut, it keeps flying instead.

FRAMING RULE
While the camera passes the cliff on the right, the left half of the picture stays completely open — flat mirror water, drifting mist, empty sky, no land and no object at all. While it passes the snow dome on the left, the right half stays completely open in the same way.

SPEED
The forward speed rises and falls on a smooth ease-in ease-out curve: fastest in the middle of the turns, slowest while passing each headland. The changes are gradual, never sudden. At its slowest the camera is still drifting forward — it never stops, never freezes, never hovers and never flies backwards.

TECHNICAL
Photorealistic 3D render, cinematic product-commercial look, 35mm lens, 1920x1080 or higher, 16:9, 24 fps. Every single frame must be sharp enough to stand alone as a still: almost no motion blur, no shutter smear, no grain, no vignette, no lens flare, no depth-of-field blur, no chromatic aberration. Constant exposure and white balance. No zoom, no focal-length change, no handheld shake.

AVOID: cuts, jump cuts, cross-fades, dissolves, a second shot, an establishing shot followed by another shot, montage, b-roll, the camera teleporting or re-framing; text, letters, numbers, subtitles, watermarks, logos, people, animals, boats, birds, aircraft, buildings; the tooth or podium returning after two seconds; camera shake, whip pans, orbiting, spinning, barrel rolls, sudden yaw; the camera stopping, freezing or flying backwards; morphing or warping terrain, headlands that change shape or swap sides; land or objects on the half of the picture that must stay open; an empty picture with no headland between the two passes; cartoon style, oversaturated colour, warm, golden or orange tint, night; any landscape visible through the closing fog, thin fog at the end, the fog clearing again.
