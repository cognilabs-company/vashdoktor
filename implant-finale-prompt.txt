ONE CONTINUOUS 10-SECOND SHOT of a lower jaw and a single dental implant. One camera, one subject, no cut, no edit, no dissolve, no montage, no scene change, no second shot. The picture runs without interruption from the first frame to the last.

THE FIRST FRAME IS THE ATTACHED IMAGE. Begin exactly there and move on from it. Do not re-invent the scene, do not re-frame it, do not change what is in it.

THE THREE RULES THAT MATTER MOST
1. NO PERSON. There is no face, no lips, no tongue, no cheeks, no head and no body anywhere in this shot at any moment. It is a jaw alone in dark space, and nothing else. It never becomes a person and a person never appears.
2. SCALE. The implant is ONE TOOTH. Its ceramic crown is exactly as wide and as tall as the natural teeth beside the gap. It is never bigger than one tooth, never the size of the jaw, never a giant object. If it looks larger than its neighbours, the shot is wrong.
3. ONE MOVE. The implant goes straight down into the waiting socket and seats there like the tooth that belongs in it. It does not tilt, swing, spin fast, bounce, or approach from the side. No tool, no drill, no tweezers, no gloves, no hands are ever in frame.

THE SCENE — unchanged for the whole shot
A human lower jaw arch alone in empty space, seen from slightly above at a three-quarter angle. Natural white teeth in smooth healthy pale pink-grey gum. One molar is missing from the lower row and a clean, tooth-sized empty socket waits in its place. Above that socket hangs a single dental implant, upright, tip down: a wide off-white ceramic molar crown, a short pale grey tapered abutment, and a light grey titanium root with smooth rounded spiral threads narrowing to a rounded point. Matte-to-satin, no chrome, no engraving, no text. The implant keeps this exact shape and these exact proportions for the whole shot.

HOW THE CAMERA MOVES
The camera only ever eases slowly BACKWARD, in a straight line, away from the jaw. It never swings, whips, sweeps or snaps sideways, never orbits, never rotates on the spot, never changes height abruptly.
At every instant, both halves of the picture move at the same rate — never one side sweeping past while the other stands still.
The pull-back is one smooth continuous ease: the speed never more than doubles and never more than halves over any one second, and it never changes in a single step.
The camera is still moving, however slightly, at every instant including the last second. The final second is a slow settle, not a freeze.

THE NEW TOOTH IS THE SUBJECT
The empty socket, and then the tooth that fills it, stays near the centre of the frame from the first frame to the last, and is always in focus.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — constant throughout
Deep blue-black background (#0a141d) with a soft cool haze. Pale blue-white key light from the upper left. A soft cyan rim (#8fc7d4) separating the jaw and implant from the dark. No warm, golden or orange tint at any point. No table, no surface, no stand, no shadow floor, no room, no props, no logo — the jaw floats in dark space. Exposure and white balance constant from the first frame to the last. The teeth stay the same natural white all the way through and never glow, sparkle or flash.

TECHNICAL
Photorealistic 3D render, cinematic product-commercial look, 50mm lens, 16:9, 24 fps, 3840x2160 if available, otherwise 1920x1080 — never lower. Every single frame must be sharp enough to stand alone as a still: almost no motion blur, no shutter smear, no grain, no vignette, no lens flare, no chromatic aberration, and never so shallow a depth of field that the new tooth goes soft. No zoom, no focal-length change, no handheld shake.
