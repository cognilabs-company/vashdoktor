THE MOVE
The shot opens on empty space — a deep blue-black void with a soft cool haze in it and nothing else in frame. The implant drops gently into the top of the picture, upright and unhurried, as if lowered on an invisible thread, and keeps descending through the middle of the frame. As it comes down, a jaw rises into the lower part of the picture to meet it: a clean, premium arch of natural white teeth in smooth pale gum, one gap waiting in the lower row. The implant descends into that gap and seats itself into the arch, and the gum closes softly around it so the new tooth sits level with its neighbours and is indistinguishable from them.

Still in the same unbroken move, the camera begins to draw back. The arch turns very slightly toward the lens and the rest of the face comes into view around it — the jaw was always part of a person. A young woman in her late twenties, fair skin, clear smooth complexion, soft natural make-up, hair simply tied back. The take ends with her held in a calm, steady frame, still smiling, the camera at rest.

The tooth is the subject the whole way through. It stays close to the middle of the picture from the moment it drops in until the end, and it is the last thing that is still sharp and centred when the shot settles — the face is revealed around it, not instead of it.

HOW THE CAMERA MOVES — this is the most important part of the shot
The camera only ever moves slowly BACKWARD, away from the subject, and very slightly upward as the face is revealed. It must never swing, whip, sweep or snap sideways, and it must never rotate on the spot.
At every instant, both halves of the picture must move at the same rate. The left side and the right side of the frame travel at the same speed — never one side sweeping past while the other stands still.
Speed changes are gradual and continuous, on a smooth ease-in ease-out curve. Over any one second the speed never more than doubles and never more than halves, and it never changes in a single step.
The camera is still moving, however slowly, at every instant including the last second. The final second is not a frozen image: it is a slow settle, not a stop.
The reveal of the face is a PULL-BACK, not a morph. The jaw does not melt, stretch or transform into a person; the camera simply widens and the person is already there around it.
