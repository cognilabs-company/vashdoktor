ONE CONTINUOUS 10-SECOND SHOT of a lower jaw and a single dental implant. One camera, one subject, no cut, no edit, no dissolve, no montage, no scene change, no second shot. The picture runs without interruption from the first frame to the last.

THE FIRST FRAME IS THE ATTACHED IMAGE. Begin exactly there and move on from it. Do not re-invent the scene, do not re-frame it, do not change what is in it.

THE FOUR RULES THAT MATTER MOST
1. EVERYTHING MOVES DOWNWARD, NOTHING EVER MOVES UPWARD. The implant comes down into the socket and stops there. Nothing in this shot ever rises, grows, sprouts, emerges, pushes up out of the gum, or travels upward for any reason at any moment. A tooth growing up out of the jaw is the single worst thing that can happen in this shot.
2. THE IMPLANT STOPS AND STAYS STOPPED. Once it is seated it is completely motionless for the rest of the take. It does not sink further, rise, settle, adjust, wobble or grow. After it comes to rest the only thing in the picture that moves is the camera.
3. THE CROWN NEVER GOES UNDER THE GUM. Only the metal threaded root enters the socket. The white ceramic crown stays above the gum and visible every single frame, from the first to the last. It never disappears below the gum and it never comes back out.
4. SCALE. The implant is ONE TOOTH. Its ceramic crown is exactly as wide and as tall as the natural teeth beside the gap. It is never bigger than one tooth, never the size of the jaw. If it looks larger than its neighbours, the shot is wrong.

NO PERSON. There is no face, no lips, no tongue, no cheeks, no head and no body anywhere in this shot at any moment. It is a jaw alone in dark space, and nothing else. It never becomes a person and a person never appears.

THE SCENE — unchanged for the whole shot
A human lower jaw arch alone in empty space, seen from slightly above at a three-quarter angle. Natural white teeth in smooth healthy pale pink-grey gum. One molar is missing from the lower row and a clean, tooth-sized empty socket waits in its place. Above that socket hangs a single dental implant, upright, tip down: a wide off-white ceramic molar crown, a short pale grey tapered abutment, and a light grey titanium root with smooth rounded spiral threads narrowing to a rounded point. Matte-to-satin, no chrome, no engraving, no text. The implant keeps this exact shape and these exact proportions for the whole shot, and moves as one rigid object — the crown, abutment and root never move separately from each other.

HOW DEEP IT GOES — exactly
The metal root goes into the socket until it is fully buried. The abutment and the crown do not enter the gum. At rest, the crown sits proud of the gum exactly as much as the neighbouring molars do, and its chewing surface is level with theirs. That is the end position, reached by descending to it and then stopping.

HOW THE CAMERA MOVES
The camera only ever eases slowly BACKWARD, in a straight line, away from the jaw. It never swings, whips, sweeps or snaps sideways, never orbits, never rotates on the spot, never changes height abruptly.
At every instant, both halves of the picture move at the same rate — never one side sweeping past while the other stands still.
The pull-back is one smooth continuous ease: the speed never more than doubles and never more than halves over any one second, and it never changes in a single step.
The camera is still moving, however slightly, at every instant including the last second. The final second is a slow settle, not a freeze.

THE NEW TOOTH IS THE SUBJECT
The empty socket, and then the tooth that fills it, stays near the centre of the frame from the first frame to the last, and is always in focus.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — constant throughout
Deep blue-black background (#0a141d) with a soft cool haze. Pale blue-white key light from the upper left. A soft cyan rim (#8fc7d4) separating the jaw and implant from the dark. No warm, golden or orange tint at any point. No table, no surface, no stand, no shadow floor, no room, no props, no logo — the jaw floats in dark space. Exposure and white balance constant from the first frame to the last. The teeth stay the same natural white all the way through and never glow, sparkle or flash.

TECHNICAL
Photorealistic 3D render, cinematic product-commercial look, 50mm lens, 16:9, 24 fps, 3840x2160 if available, otherwise 1920x1080 — never lower. Every single frame must be sharp enough to stand alone as a still: almost no motion blur, no shutter smear, no grain, no vignette, no lens flare, no chromatic aberration, and never so shallow a depth of field that the new tooth goes soft. No zoom, no focal-length change, no handheld shake.

AVOID
A tooth growing, sprouting, rising, emerging, pushing up or unfolding out of the gum; anything at all travelling upward in the frame; the implant rising back up after it lands; the implant bobbing, settling, sinking further or adjusting after it stops; the crown disappearing below the gum and reappearing; a tooth forming, materialising or assembling itself in the socket; time-lapse growth of any kind.
The implant tilting, swinging, spinning fast, bouncing, screwing itself in with visible rotation, approaching from the side, or missing the socket; the crown, abutment and root separating or moving independently; the implant changing shape, proportions or finish, or turning into a natural tooth with roots; more than one implant; more than one gap.
The implant larger than one tooth; an implant the size of the jaw or the arch; a giant implant descending onto a small jaw; the implant out of proportion with the teeth beside it.
A face, lips, tongue, cheeks, chin, nose, eyes, a head, a person, a body, skin — any part of a human being; the jaw turning into a person; an upper jaw; a full skull; a mouth opening or closing.
Cuts, jump cuts, cross-fades, dissolves, a second shot, montage, the camera teleporting or re-framing; the camera swinging sideways faster than it pulls back; whip pans, sudden yaw, a lurch or a jolt; any single-frame jump in speed or direction; the camera stopping dead; a still or frozen final second.
A clinical or surgical look; blood; gums cut open; exposed bone or a bone cutaway; drills, tweezers, tools, gloves or hands; a dental cast, denture or model on a table or stand.
Crooked, uneven, oversized, doubled or extra teeth; teeth that change shape or number during the shot.
Text, letters, numbers, subtitles, watermarks, logos, UI overlays.
Warm, golden or orange light; a bright or white background; a room, clinic or interior; cartoon style; oversaturated colour.
